# Rule checks for NZ and Australian contact centre rosters

Checked 29 October 2026. Run `npm run wfm -- compliance`. These checks find rostered shifts that look inconsistent with the rules below. They do not decide which award, agreement or contract covers a person, they do not calculate pay, and they are not legal advice. Each agent's `jurisdiction` and `award` fields say which checks apply. Set them from the employment agreement, not from a guess.

| Check | What the data shows | Rule and source | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| au-meal-5h | An agent with award `MA000023` is rostered more than 300 minutes without a meal break. | Contract Call Centres Award 2020 [MA000023] cl 14.1: "Where practicable, an employee must not be required to work for more than 5 hours without a break for a meal". Cl 14.2: the meal break is 30 to 60 minutes, unpaid. Cl 14.3 sets a 150% rate for the meal period when directed to work past 5 hours. [Fair Work award text](https://awards.fairwork.gov.au/MA000023.html) | Cl 14.4 allows up to 6 hours by majority agreement. Record that agreement before relying on it. The award covers the contract call centre industry. An in-house centre may sit under a different award with different rules. |
| au-part-time-3h | A part-time agent with award `MA000023` is rostered for less than 3 hours. | Cl 10.3: "An employer is required to roster a part-time employee for a minimum of 3 consecutive hours on any shift." | Checks rostered length only. |
| au-casual-3h-pay | A casual agent with award `MA000023` is rostered for less than 3 hours. | Cl 11.2: a casual is entitled to payment for a minimum of 3 hours on each occasion they attend work. | A short casual shift is allowed. The finding is a prompt that it pays as 3 hours. |
| au-10h-rest | Less than 10 hours between the end of one shift and the start of the next for an agent with award `MA000023`. | Cl 20.4: when overtime is necessary, arrange wherever reasonably practicable at least 10 consecutive hours off between the work of successive days, with release from duty or 150% pay where it is not given. | The clause sits in the overtime clause. The check flags every short break between rostered shifts so a person can decide whether it applies. |
| au-roster-notice | A published shift for an agent with award `MA000023` changed on the "notice" basis with less than one week's notice, or on the "emergency" basis with less than 48 hours. | Cl 13.5(a): ordinary hours once determined may be altered by one week's notice, by 48 hours' notice in an emergency, by mutual agreement, or at the employee's discretion through an employer-permitted swap. | Changes recorded as "agreed" are not flagged. Keep the evidence of agreement. |
| nz-breaks | An NZ agent's shift has fewer paid 10 minute rest breaks or unpaid 30 minute meal breaks than the minimum for the work period. | Employment Relations Act 2000 Part 6D, as set out by [Employment New Zealand](https://www.employment.govt.nz/hours-and-wages/breaks/rest-and-meal-breaks): 2 to 4 hours, one rest; over 4 to 6, one rest and one meal; over 6 to under 10, two rest and one meal; 10 to 12, three rest and one meal; over 12 to 14, three rest and two meals; over 14 to 16, four rest and two meals. | Employer and employee can agree timing, and some exemptions exist. The check counts breaks on the roster. It does not check when they are actually taken. |
| roster-unpublished | A draft shift starts within the next 7 days. | Operator policy in this base, chosen to match the one week notice idea in cl 13.5. | Change the window in a migration if your agreements say otherwise. |

To clear a finding, fix the roster: move a break or a shift with `/move-shift`, lengthen or cancel a shift, publish the roster, or record that the change was agreed. Never edit the rule to make a finding disappear. If a rule here is out of date, update this file and the check in a new migration together, with the date and source.

Not checked here: pay rates, penalty rates, overtime, leave accrual, maximum weekly hours, fatigue, and payroll records. Those belong to payroll and the employment agreements.
